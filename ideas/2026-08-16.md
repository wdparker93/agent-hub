**Idea 1: ReloadLab**

**Problem:** Handloaders — people who reload their own ammunition — track their load development data in Excel spreadsheets, paper notebooks, or nothing at all. When a competitive shooter is working up a precision load (testing charge weights from 41.0gr to 43.0gr in 0.2gr increments across multiple range sessions), they need to correlate powder charge, bullet seating depth, primer brand, brass headstamp, muzzle velocity from a chronograph, and group size at distance — across sessions weeks apart, sometimes across different rifles. No modern software does this. The dedicated apps that exist (LoadWorkup, the old Infinity Reloading software) are either abandoned, Windows-only and ugly, or basic to the point of useless. Nothing connects range session data to load recipes and surfaces "charge weight X with this primer gave the lowest ES three sessions in a row."

**Target buyer:** Precision rifle competitors (PRS/NRL), F-Class shooters, and serious handloaders who already spend $300–800/year on components alone. Not casual hunters — the data-obsessed crowd who owns a Magnetospeed chronograph and posts ladder test photos to r/longrange.

**Monetization:** One-time purchase, $49 direct download or Steam (Steam adds discoverability and handles payment in 180+ countries). A "Pro" unlock at $79 adds data export to CSV and multi-rifle comparison charts. Conservative estimate: 1,200 sales in year one = ~$59k gross. This community buys software if it looks credible — they paid for Kestrel's $600 ballistic computer without blinking.

**MVP scope:** Create and save load recipes (component fields + charge weight). Log range sessions and link them to a recipe. Input velocity strings and calculate ES/SD automatically. Plot charge weight vs. group size and vs. ES. Export session notes as PDF. Does NOT do: ballistic calculators, recoil math, inventory tracking, supplier pricing, mobile app.

**Distribution:** r/reloading (270k members), r/longrange, Sniper's Hide forum, YouTube sponsorship or demo video targeting channels like Gafuller or the Applied Ballistics crowd. PRS Facebook groups. A free tier that caps at 3 rifles gets you word-of-mouth without destroying revenue.

**Why a solo dev wins here:** This is too small and too legally adjacent to firearms for any major software company to bother. Big productivity tools ignore it. The existing competitors are abandonware. A solo dev who shoots or can talk the talk credibly will be trusted immediately in a community that is deeply skeptical of outsiders but loyal to tools that work.

**Biggest risk:** The community is vocal and opinionated — if you ship something that feels like it was made by someone who's never touched a reloading press, you'll get publicly roasted on Sniper's Hide and recovery is slow.

**Idea 2: PackRat**

**Problem:** Bedroom producers move computers, reinstall Windows, or collaborate with someone else and their Ableton/FL Studio/Bitwig project immediately breaks — red "missing sample" warnings everywhere, unresolvable plugin references, tracks that play silence. This happens to almost every producer who's worked for more than two years. The root cause: DAW projects store absolute file paths, not relative ones, and sample libraries sprawl across drives. There is no tool that acts like a package manager for audio projects — scanning a project file, inventorying every external dependency (samples, presets, impulse responses), and either flagging what's missing or packing everything into a relocatable archive. Ableton has a weak "collect all and save" feature; FL Studio's equivalent is fragile; nothing works cross-DAW. Producers waste hours hunting files before a session, collaboration, or album submission.

**Target buyer:** Bedroom producers who've been making music for 3+ years, have accumulated 50+ projects across multiple drives, and release or sell music (on Bandcamp, DistroKid, or as sample packs). Specifically the person who just got a new laptop and is staring at a broken project for a release due next week.

**Monetization:** $29 one-time purchase, direct download (Mac + Windows). Optional $9/mo "Vault" tier adds cloud backup of the packed project archives via your own S3/Backblaze bucket (you provide the API key — you're not storing their audio). 500 one-time sales = $14.5k; 150 Vault subscribers = $16.2k ARR. Totals are modest but very achievable in a community that spends freely on plugins.

**MVP scope:** Point it at a project file (starting with Ableton .als format — largest user base). It parses the XML, lists every referenced sample path, flags missing files, and offers to copy all found files into a /samples subfolder alongside the project, rewriting paths. That's it. Does NOT do: plugin dependency tracking (too complex for v1), FL Studio or Logic support, cloud backup, DAW launching.

**Distribution:** r/edmproduction, r/ableton, r/WeAreTheMusicMakers. A demo GIF of a broken project becoming a clean archived folder will travel far on Twitter/X music producer circles. One YouTube video from a mid-tier producer (10k–100k subs) closes it. Cold DM 20 producers who've complained publicly about missing samples — they exist and are easy to find.

**Why a solo dev wins here:** Ableton won't fix this properly because it would require them to solve it for every third-party sample library simultaneously — a political and technical nightmare. Plugin companies don't care because it's not their problem. A solo dev can build a focused, cross-DAW tool in 6 weeks of evenings because DAW project files are XML or documented binary formats.

**Biggest risk:** Ableton ships a meaningfully better "collect and save" in the next major version and removes 40% of the urgency for their user base, which is the dominant segment.

**Idea 3: DriftWatch**

**Problem:** Solo developers and indie SaaS builders integrate 5–20 third-party REST APIs (Stripe, Twilio, Plaid, Notion, OpenAI, whatever) and get burned when those APIs change quietly. Response fields appear, disappear, or change type. Rate limits shift. Auth behavior changes. You find out when production breaks at 2am, not when the change was deployed. Existing solutions (Optic, Bump.sh, Treblle) are aimed at teams managing their *own* APIs, require OpenAPI specs you may not have, or are priced for engineering organizations. There's no lightweight "I depend on other people's APIs — tell me when they drift" tool designed for a solo developer's 10-minute setup budget.

**Target buyer:** Indie SaaS developers and technical freelancers who run production integrations against APIs they don't control and have been burned by silent breaking changes. The person whose Stripe webhook integration broke because a field was renamed and who spent 4 hours debugging before reading a buried changelog entry.

**Monetization:** SaaS subscription. $15/mo for up to 10 monitored endpoints, $29/mo for 30 endpoints with Slack/webhook alerting. No free tier — trial only (14 days). At 80 paying customers average $20/mo = $1,600 MRR = $19.2k ARR. Boring but real. This is a pure "saves me money" value prop so willingness to pay is high relative to price.

**MVP scope:** User adds an API endpoint URL + an example auth header. DriftWatch calls it on a schedule (hourly/daily), stores the JSON response schema (field names, types, nesting), and emails you when the schema changes. Dashboard shows a diff view: "field `customer.address.zip` was present yesterday, absent today." Does NOT do: OpenAPI import, semantic diffing of behavior, latency monitoring, mobile app, team features.

**Distribution:** Hacker News Show HN (this is exactly the audience), Indie Hackers, r/selfhosted, dev.to post titled something like "I got burned by 3 silent API changes in 6 months, so I built this." Cold outreach to technical indie hackers who've publicly complained about API reliability.

**Why a solo dev wins here:** The enterprise API monitoring space is full of tools aimed at platform teams with complex observability stacks. Nobody is building for the solo dev who just needs a smoke alarm, not a full SIEM. Small scope is the product — a big company would bloat this into an observability platform within 18 months.

**Biggest risk:** The most painful integrations (Plaid, financial APIs) have dynamic responses that make schema-diffing noisy, leading to alert fatigue and churn. You need smart debouncing from day one or the core value collapses.

**Idea 4: ChronicPaw**

**Problem:** Cats with chronic conditions — kidney disease (CKD affects 1 in 3 cats over 10), hyperthyroidism, diabetes, IBD — require ongoing monitoring: weight trends, appetite scoring, litter box output, medication timing, bloodwork values over time. Their owners track this in Notes apps, Google Sheets, or memory. When they walk into a vet appointment they can't answer "when exactly did the vomiting start again?" or "has his weight been trending down or did it just drop this week?" Vets make treatment decisions on incomplete longitudinal data. Generic pet apps (Pawtrack, PetDesk) are appointment schedulers and vaccine reminders — none are designed for the medical management of a cat living with a long-term condition. This owner is spending $2,000–8,000/year on veterinary care and would pay for a tool that makes those visits more effective.

**Target buyer:** Owners of cats (or dogs secondarily) with diagnosed chronic conditions who are already engaged in active management — giving subcutaneous fluids at home, dosing methimazole twice daily, doing at-home glucose curves. Found in r/CKDCAT (47k members), r/felinediabetes, and Facebook groups for cat CKD (tens of thousands of members, extremely active).

**Monetization:** $4.99/month or $34.99/year mobile app (iOS + Android via React Native). One "premium" unlock at $9.99/yr adds vet report export (formatted PDF of the past 30/90 days of data, ready to hand to a vet). 400 annual subscribers = $14k ARR; 1,000 = $35k ARR. Small but the community is tightly networked and retention on a tool tracking a sick pet is extremely high — they use it until the cat dies, which unfortunately is often years.

**MVP scope:** Add a pet profile with a diagnosis tag. Log daily: weight (oz/g), appetite (1–5 scale), vomiting (yes/no + count), litter box (normal/abnormal). Log medications with doses and timing. Log vet visits with bloodwork values (BUN, creatinine, T4, etc.) as simple key-value entries. Show simple trend charts per metric over 30/90 days. Does NOT do: medication reminders in v1, vet portal integration, AI analysis, multi-pet household management.

**Distribution:** Post in r/CKDCAT and r/felinediabetes as a genuine member (or reach out to mods). These communities are desperate for better tools and have extremely high word-of-mouth velocity — a pinned recommendation from a trusted community member is worth more than any ad. The CKD Cat Facebook group alone has 30,000 members.

**Why a solo dev wins here:** Big pet health companies (Zoetis, Idexx) are focused on veterinary clinic software, not pet owner empowerment. Consumer pet apps are funded to be broad ("all pets, all features") which means no depth for chronic condition management. A solo dev can build something that exactly matches the r/CKDCAT community's mental model because you can spend a week just reading their posts before writing a line of code.

**Biggest risk:** Scope creep pressure from a community that has extremely specific and varying needs across different conditions — CKD cat owners want different things than diabetic cat owners. You need to hold the line on a single condition type for v1 or you'll never ship.

**Idea 5: LoreCheck**

**Problem:** Hard sci-fi writers, Traveller/Cepheus RPG game masters, and sci-fi game designers maintain sprawling fictional universes — and contradict themselves constantly. Travel times between star systems change chapter to chapter. A species described as "unable to digest carbohydrates" is later eating bread. A ship established as 200 meters long is later docking in a bay described as 80 meters wide. Scrivener is a writing tool, not a consistency engine. World Anvil is a wiki builder aimed at fantasy and so generic it requires enormous setup with no intelligence. There is no tool that lets you define the *rules* of your universe (physics constraints, species traits, technology tiers, canonical distances) and then flags when your own writing violates them. The only existing "consistency checking" is ctrl+F and a long memory.

**Target buyer:** Serious sci-fi writers working on series (not one-shots), hard sci-fi specifically — people who care that their FTL system is internally consistent — and GMs running long-form Traveller, Cepheus Engine, or homebrew hard sci-fi campaigns. These people buy Scrivener ($60), World Anvil subscriptions ($9–$99/mo), and specialized reference books. They are not casual.

**Monetization:** $12/month or $89/year SaaS (web app). Positioned above Scrivener, below World Anvil Pro. 200 annual subscribers = $17.8k ARR; 500 = $44.5k ARR. Credibility in the hard sci-fi writing community unlocks affiliate potential with self-publishing newsletters and Traveller community partnerships.

**MVP scope:** A "Codex" where you define entities (ships, species, locations, technologies) with typed attributes (numerical ranges, boolean traits, enum values, free-text). A "Rules" layer where you assert constraints ("travel from Sol to Alpha Centauri at Jump-2 takes 2 weeks," "Keth cannot eat anything with glucose"). A document importer (paste text or upload .docx) that scans for references to defined entities and flags potential constraint violations — not AI hallucination, just pattern matching against your own definitions. Does NOT do: full manuscript editing, AI writing assistance, collaborative multi-author features, visual star map generation.

**Distribution:** r/scifiwriting, r/worldbuilding filtered to hard sci-fi, the Traveller RPG community (Citizens of the Imperium forum, r/traveller — a deeply devoted niche), sci-fi writing podcasts (Writing Excuses adjacent communities). A "hard sci-fi consistency" angle is a genuine content hook — one good blog post or YouTube video titled "How I stopped contradicting myself across a 400k-word series" drives organic search and community shares.

**Why a solo dev wins here:** World Anvil is built by a team, funded, and optimizing for breadth and fantasy aesthetics — they will never niche down to hard sci-fi constraint logic. Scrivener is focused on prose organization, not semantic consistency. The hard sci-fi community is small enough that a big company can't justify building for it, but large enough and opinionated enough that they'll pay and evangelize loudly for a tool built specifically for them.

**Biggest risk:** The constraint-flagging system produces too many false positives (or misses too much) because natural language matching against defined entities is genuinely hard without heavy NLP investment — and if the core feature feels unreliable, the whole value proposition collapses.
